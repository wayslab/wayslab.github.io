      text: "Wireless channel measurements across multiple spatial directions are crucial for AI-driven applications, such as RF digital twins and integrated communication and sensing. However, collecting channel data across large scenes is labor-intensive. Wireless NeRFs address this challenge by learning propagation behavior from sparse measurements and synthesizing channel spatial spectrum magnitude at unseen locations. However, existing wireless NeRFs inherit dense volumetric sampling from vision NeRFs, which requires substantial computation. This paper asks whether such dense sampling is necessary for predicting magnitudes of the wireless spatial spectrum. We empirically show that wireless NeRFs are over-parameterized for this task and introduce SV-INGP, a sparse volumetric sampling variant of Instant Neural Graphics Primitives (INGP). Across real-world and simulated datasets, SV-INGP matches the median Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) of the NeRF2 baseline while reducing training time by 184x. These results generalize across LoS and NLoS scenes, sub-6 and millimeter-wave frequencies, and antenna array tapering configurations, suggesting a simpler and more efficient design path for RF digital twins."
